Honesty, Integrity, and Learner-Centered Design

Integrity isn't a finishing touch in instructional design — it's the foundation that protects learner trust, from how I define a problem to how I report results.

DS
D. Samuel • May 2026 • 2 min read

Honesty and integrity matter in instructional design because learning influences what people believe they can do — and what organizations rely on them to do. In my work, integrity shows up in how I define the problem, how I design assessments, and how I communicate results. I avoid "busy" activities that look engaging but don't measure real learning. Instead, I aim for alignment, so objectives, instruction, practice, and assessment connect in a way that is fair and meaningful for learners.

Honesty through reflection and revision

I also practice honesty through reflection and revision. When feedback or results show that something isn't working, I treat that as useful information — not a failure. I adjust the design, clarify directions, strengthen practice opportunities, and improve supports so learners have a better path to success.

When results show something isn't working, I treat that as useful information — not a failure.

This connects to one of my professional strengths: being willing to analyze what is not working and revise with purpose, instead of moving forward with a design simply because it is already finished.

The strengths I bring

My professional strengths include organization, intentional planning, learner-centered thinking, and alignment. With 12 years of experience in elementary education, I have learned how important it is to break information into manageable steps, anticipate where learners may struggle, and create supports that build confidence. Through my instructional design coursework, I have also strengthened my ability to use models such as ADDIE, apply backward design, write measurable objectives, and design assessments that reflect real learning.

The values that guide me

The Canyon Center for Character Education Professional Competencies emphasizes ethical decision-making, responsibility, and reflective growth. Those values guide how I protect learner trust, respect diverse perspectives, and design with care. Ultimately, I want my portfolio to reflect not only what I create, but how I create it — thoughtfully, responsibly, and with the learner at the center.
